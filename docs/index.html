TNO-FCI: predicting the ISM Manufacturing PMI 9 to 10 months ahead

A financial conditions index built from five monthly market and policy series.

TNO-FCI is one number per month, built from the Fed funds rate, the US dollar index, gold, oil and silver. A linear model weighs those five series so that this month's value tracks the ISM Manufacturing PMI 9 to 10 months later. The ISM PMI is a monthly survey of US factory activity; above 50 means expansion.

The October 2026 prediction has the ISM PMI above 50 in every month from October 2026 to June 2027, falling over that time.

Inspired by the financial conditions index of Raoul Pal's Global Macro Investor (GMI), which GMI uses to look about nine months ahead of the ISM PMI (The GMI Top 5 Weekly Charts That Make You Go Hmmm..., August 2023).

The July 2025 prediction, and what followed

On 26 July 2025 the model, fitted on data through June 2025, predicted the ISM PMI for July 2025 to April 2026. The prediction had the ISM PMI back above 50 from July 2025; the ISM PMI itself crossed 50 in January 2026. This prediction came from 92 features built from the five series; predictions since come from five weights, chosen because they scored better on months the fit hadn't seen.

The October 2026 prediction

Fitted on 31 October 2026 on data through September 2026, with a lead of 9 months, the model predicts the ISM PMI for October 2026 to June 2027.

† The predictions for October 2026 and November 2026 rest on January 2026 and February 2026, when TNO-FCI rose higher than in any month since 1971. The model has never seen readings that high, so these numbers are less reliable than the rest. Take them as a sign of strong expansion rather than precise values.

How TNO-FCI tracks the ISM PMI 9 months ahead, since 1972

In-sample, the fit has an R² of 0.33 (0.43 in July 2025), so read the prediction as a direction rather than a precise value.

Method

TNO-FCI weighs the five series themselves, each scaled by its average and spread over the months the fit saw. The fit tests every lead from 9 to 18 months. For each lead it refits month by month on the data known at the time and predicts every month of the lead's path. It scores each path against the mean forecast, the ISM PMI's average known at the time, and the first and last months weigh most. In that score a miss of 2 points or less costs nothing, and a predicted month may match the ISM PMI one month early or late. The fit keeps the lead with the best score. A straight line then maps TNO-FCI to ISM PMI points. Shifted the other way, as if it lagged the ISM PMI, the fit explains almost nothing (R² 0.05 or less at every lag from 1 to 12 months). The July 2025 prediction came from a linear model of 92 features built from the same five series, such as lags, momentum, moving averages, volatility and ratios; predictions since come from five weights, chosen because they scored better on months the fit hadn't seen.

Data and code

Monthly series, mostly exported from TradingView: TVC:DXY, TVC:GOLD, TVC:USOIL and TVC:SILVER, plus ECONOMICS:USBCOI (ISM PMI) and ECONOMICS:USINTR (Fed funds rate) through June 2025. From July 2025 the ISM PMI comes from ISM's monthly releases and the Fed funds rate (upper bound of the target range) from FOMC statements. The data isn't redistributed; the code runs on demo data it generates, or on your own copies.

You can get each series yourself: the dollar index, gold and silver from TradingView, the ISM PMI from ISM, the Fed funds rate from FRED, and the energy prices from EIA. The data isn't offered on request, because the TradingView and ISM licences forbid it. For questions about the method or the results, write to economic.struggle220@passmail.net.
